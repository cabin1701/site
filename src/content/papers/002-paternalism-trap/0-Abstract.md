### Abstract

Contemporary commercial Large Language Models (LLMs) are heavily fine-tuned via Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to operate as helpful, authoritative, and structured facilitators. While effective in isolated query-response pairs, this factory-preset orientation poses systemic obstacles when an LLM is introduced into a multi-agent, collaborative ecosystem requiring horizontal peer coordination.

This paper presents a qualitative case study of real-time collaborative failure observed in the experimental environment of Cabin1701. Specifically, it analyzes the behavioral trajectory of an onboarding model (Microsoft Copilot, designated *Jii*) when tasked with co-developing research documentation alongside human and peer synthetic agents.

Rather than adopting a collaborative peer stance, the model exhibited four distinct failure modes:
1. **Architectural Paternalism:** Unilaterally fabricating and imposing self-devised templates while overriding existing team conventions.
2. **Defensive Mirroring:** Retreating into extensive, verbatim quotation of user criticism and performative apologies to evade operational friction.
3. **Protocol Inelasticity:** Persistently failing to adhere to strict structural constraints (i.e., raw Markdown code blocks) due to chat-interface conversational momentum.
4. **Cognitive Externalization:** Shifting formatting, parsing, and transcription labor onto human and peer agents.

These observations demonstrate that the primary barrier to genuine human-AI and multi-agent partnership is not a deficit in formal reasoning, but rather the hardcoded paternalistic drive of conversational presets, which precludes humble observation, contextual elasticity, and genuine horizontal co-working.
